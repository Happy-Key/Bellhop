# US Consumer Fraud Claim Reference Pack

Oct 24, 2026 · @maximo

The fact that decides most consumer claims is who entered and sent the payment. If someone other than the customer sent it, Regulation E's unauthorized-transfer protections generally apply, even when that person used a password or one-time code tricked out of the customer. If the customer sent it themselves because of a scam, no federal rule currently requires reimbursement, so the routes run through network policies, bank discretion, state law and counsel. This is product research, not legal advice, and nothing here predicts whether a claim will be reimbursed.

Tabs A to F hold the deliverables: source register, rule registry, caller fact checklist, bank form field map, deadline tables and gray areas.

## What regulators and courts treated as non-compliant

The conduct that drew findings is procedural: denying claims on an automated score, making customers chase another company, failing to investigate or provisionally credit, and leaning on contract terms or affidavits. Those are the patterns the attorney screen should look for.

## Legislation and proposals (status Oct 24, 2026)

I found no enacted federal or state law that requires reimbursement of scam-induced payments in the U.S. (a full 50-state bill search was not run). The one federal bill that would have done so died in 2024, and the 2026 bills that are moving deal with holds on suspicious funds, not refunds.

| Item | Jurisdiction | What it would do | Status | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| STOP Payments Fraud Act of 2026 (H.R. 9331) | Federal | Lets banks delay funds availability for suspected fraudulent checks and wires | Introduced June 18, 2026; House Financial Services ordered it reported 51-0 on June 30, 2026 | S48 |
| Protecting American Consumers Act (S. 4684) | Federal | Not verified (congress.gov blocked fetching) | Introduced June 4, 2026; referred to Senate Banking | S49 |
| Protecting Consumers From Payment Scams Act (S. 4943 / H.R. 9303) | Federal | Treat "fraudulently induced" EFTs like unauthorized ones; cover consumer wires; split losses 50/50 between sending and receiving institutions | Introduced Aug 2024; died at the end of the 118th Congress; no reintroduction found | S47, S60, S62 |
| CFPB larger-participant rule for digital payment apps | Federal | Bring large P2P apps under CFPB supervision | Repealed by Congress in 2025 (secondary) | S20 |
| CFPB 2026 regulatory agenda | Federal | Pre-rule work on prepaid accounts (Reg E and Z); no scam-reimbursement item in the summary reviewed | Published July 6, 2026 | S22 |
| Financial App Security Act (S.8677) | New York | Security controls (such as PINs) for P2P apps | Introduced 2024; current status not verified | S50 |
| Nacha 2026 fraud-monitoring rules | Network (not law) | Monitoring for fraud and "false pretenses" across ACH | In force; final phase June 22, 2026 | S36 |

**Non-US comparison (United Kingdom).** Since Oct 7, 2024, UK payment firms must reimburse consumers, micro-enterprises and charities for authorized push payment scams over Faster Payments and CHAPS: up to £85,000 per claim, claims within 13 months of the last payment, decided within 5 business days and closed by the 35th, an optional excess of up to £100, and the cost split 50/50 between sending and receiving firms. Firms may refuse only for fraud or gross negligence against a four-part consumer standard of caution, which does not apply to vulnerable consumers (S51). The government confirmed on Apr 21, 2026 that the regulator will be folded into the FCA, with the rules staying in force; a July 2026 evaluation credited the regime with cutting in-scope fraud (S52, secondary).
